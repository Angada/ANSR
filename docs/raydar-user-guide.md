# RayDar — how to use it

*Talent Trend Radar · qansr.thekettleblack.in*

RayDar tells you what to publish. It reads what job seekers are actually
watching, asking and complaining about, and gives you ranked content ideas with
the evidence behind each one.

It does **not** write the finished piece. It briefs; your writers write.

---

## Running a sweep — five minutes

**1 · Demand Setting**
Pick **one content series** — 1Up, Skill Up, Interview Lab or Resume Lab. One at
a time; picking another switches to it.

Then fine-tune the themes beneath it. **For the sharpest results, run one theme
at a time** — a sweep aimed at one subject reads far deeper than one spread
across twelve.

Optional: paste SEO research into Feed 02, or type a line in *Your brief*
("focus on people who were just laid off").

Press **Run the sweep**.

**2 · Sweep**
This is where it lands when it finishes. It shows the evidence:

- **What went into this sweep** — the themes armed, the exact search terms
  fired, the research in play, your brief
- **What the feed returned** — collected, kept, filtered, and *why* each item
  was dropped
- **Gap analysis** — what people asked versus what already answers them, with
  the real questions quoted from the comments
- **Why these ideas scored what they did** — in plain English

**3 · Content Ideas**
The ideas themselves. Each carries a title, the angle, and the justification.

Mark each one **Used**, **Save** or **Reject** (with a reason). It then drops out
of the list into a *done* drawer, so the list shrinks as you work.

---

## The other tabs

**Batches** — every sweep you've run. Open one to revisit its evidence and ideas.
Also where you purge old sweeps (it previews first, and never deletes a sweep
holding an idea you marked Used or Saved).

**Library** — every idea ever generated, across every sweep. Filter by what you
decided, why you rejected it, which series it belongs to, or which sweep it came
from. Nothing you mark is lost.

**Settings** — the dials, all editable without a developer: how far back to look,
how many videos per term, minimum video length, minimum views, blocked channels,
allowed languages, and the ranking weights.

---

## Editing your themes

*Demand Setting → ⚙ edit concepts*

Describe a theme in your own words — *"Resume tips and fixes, framing over
content"* — then press **✨ Work out the logic from this**. RayDar proposes the
search terms, where it routes, and what to ignore. **You confirm before anything
saves.** The configuration stays yours.

---

## Two things worth knowing

**The quota.** A YouTube search costs 100 units against 10,000 a day. Roughly a
dozen sweeps daily at the current settings. Re-running the same theme within 24
hours is served from cache and costs nothing. If the allowance runs out, the
results page says so plainly — *"youtube — out of quota"* — rather than showing
an empty result.

**It tells you when it can't.** Where RayDar lacks grounding it says so instead
of guessing: no feed keys means the run is labelled *LLM-only*; dropped items
show their reason; a sweep that produces nothing explains why. Treat a silent
empty page as a bug worth reporting, because it shouldn't happen.

---

## If something looks wrong

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| *"youtube — out of quota"* | The daily allowance is spent. Resets ~12:30pm IST. |
| *"key rejected"* | The API key itself needs attention (Admin → Integrations). |
| *"The sweep finished but produced no ideas"* | The message names the cause and where to look. |
| Videos that feel off-topic | Tighten the theme's search terms in ⚙ edit concepts. |

Anything else, send a screenshot of the **What the feed returned** panel — it
carries the numbers needed to diagnose it.
